How this was measured

Source: . Each airport's hourly reports are read on the eight synoptic hours only (00, 03 … 21 UTC, which is 2am, 5am … 11pm on a summer day in the Bay), and each reading is filed under its Pacific date, so a day is one calendar day from the small hours to the evening. Only those hours, because 1969–1972 survive only at 3-hourly resolution and an hourly year would otherwise get three times the chances to catch fog. NOAA's older database stops in August 2025, so 2025 and 2026 come from its successor, GHCNh, which gives the same answer as the older one to a tenth of a point on the summers both cover. "Low cloud" is a ceiling at or below 400 m (about 1,300 ft), the marine layer the Bay calls fog; fog measured by visibility is not counted. SFO's record switches from the old SAO format to METAR reports in July ; the summers either side of it differ by points.
