# Is the Bay's summer fog disappearing?

**<https://matmat202.github.io/fog-decades/>**

Every summer day (June to August) at San Francisco International and Oakland International
since 1948, read from NOAA's hourly airport observations. Each square is one day, shaded by
how many of its eight 3-hourly readings had a cloud ceiling at or below 400 m.

## What the record shows

At SFO, summer low cloud fell from 20.6% of readings in the 1950s to 16.2% in the 1960s.
Since 1960 it has barely moved. Oakland, the comparison station, shows the same step in the
1950s and has risen a little since. So the decline is a single step between the 1950s and
the 1960s, seen at both airports, and nothing after it. The record cannot tell whether that
step was the weather or a change in how observers worked, and the page says so.

## Where the numbers come from

NOAA NCEI's [Integrated Surface Database](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/land-based-station/integrated-surface-database)
up to 2024, and its successor [GHCNh](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/global-historical-climatology-network-hourly)
from 2025. The two were checked against each other on summer 2024 before being joined (SFO
15.76% in both). Only the eight synoptic hours are used, because some early-1970s years were
recorded every 3 hours and hourly years would otherwise get eight times as many chances to
catch fog. SFO's switch to automated (METAR) reports in July 1996 changes the rate by
0.17 points, too small to matter here.
