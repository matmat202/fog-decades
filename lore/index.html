How this was scored

Every saying is a sign and an outcome. Each card compares how often the outcome happened on days with the sign against days without it, in the same season, at San Francisco International Airport. A saying holds when the gap stays above zero in 95% of re-runs that each draw whole years at random, which allows for runs of wet and dry years. It runs backwards when the gap stays below zero, and is no better than chance otherwise. "Rain" is any measurable amount in an hour; traces count as dry.

The barometer card is the check on the method: falling pressure ahead of a storm is physics, so a method that could not see it could not see anything. Dew and clear skies are judged from the 6 am temperature, dew point, wind and cloud, not from the grass itself. Earthquakes are USGS's catalogue of magnitude 4 and up within 100 km of the airport ( days).

Source: NOAA's Integrated Surface Database, hourly reports from SFO, ( left out as incomplete), and the USGS earthquake catalogue. One station is not the Bay: the airport is sunnier than the city and drier than the hills. Built .
